### Changed
- A judgment of an obligation binding counts only against the obligation text it was written on. `manifest_put` stamps `judged_on` (the plan hash each judgment field, `code_validity`, was written against) and folds it per key; re-sending `design_assessment` after a re-plan refreshes the design only, a `code_validity` judged on the old text reads `unverified` in the coverage map until it is re-sent. Bindings written before this change keep their reading until their first new write.
- A fragment that sends `judged_on` is refused with the one to leave out ("leave it out, the code writes it"); a binding `obligation_hash` that equals the plan's, or that arrives with no plan to compare, is dropped like any other sent hash instead of being stored; an invalid migration-only `renamed_from` is refused ("it is the migration's").
- The S5 precheck names a bound obligation whose binding carries a judgment written against another text (the plan changed, or the binding was renamed to this id): finding `binding-stale-<id>` in the `obligations` family, with the fields and the one fragment that re-judges them. The stale-binding clause leaves the `obligation_refs` finding, so the door is stated once.
- `migrate_artifacts` renaming a legacy `OB-n` binding to the plan's id no longer revives its judgments: every field is forwarded with the stamp it was judged on, a judgment not current against the new id's text counts for nothing, and the migration report names it in one `left` row per binding ("not current, re-judge: <fields>") for the automator to re-send.
- The binding contract text says `obligation_hash` and `judged_on` are the code's and that the role re-sends the fields it re-judged.
