- The probe's list of a test's checks now reads soft SCOPES: a bracketed region whose checks do not stop one another (NUnit `Assert.Multiple`, JUnit `assertAll`, AssertJ `assertSoftly` and `AutoCloseableSoftAssertions`). A scope is a row `soft_scopes` of the language's entry in `references/detectors.json` (`opens`: a regex ending on the opening bracket; `head`, optional: which checks inside are the scope's soft ones); every check inside is listed with its own key and `soft: true`, the scope's own opening is not a check, and the plan puts them in one run. The scope's end stops the test where it closes, so the plan never reads a check past it in the same run, and a stopping check inside a scope runs alone. A scope whose closing cannot be found, or that holds no check the factory reads, is no scope and a note names the line. A soft scope that ends at a CALL, not at a bracket (AssertJ `SoftAssertions softly = new SoftAssertions(); ... softly.assertAll();`, TestNG `SoftAssert sa = new SoftAssert(); ... sa.assertAll();`), is a row with a new optional field `closes`: the scope opens at the declaration `opens` matches (which then needs no trailing bracket; the loader refuses a row with neither) and ends at the first later `closes` match in the same body; `{name}` of a named group of `opens` (the variable) is put into `closes` and `head`. A variable with no closing call in its body (returned, passed on, closed in a conditional, a lambda or an after-each) opens no scope: its checks read as stopping and a note names the line.
