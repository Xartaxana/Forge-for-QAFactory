QA Factory — Architecture

This page describes QA Factory version 0.10.2. Everything below is checkable in the plugin package — skills/qa-story/SKILL.md and its phases/, agents/*.md, references/, hooks/hooks.json and the scripts under scripts/. Where a rule is enforced by code, the script that enforces it is named.

The factory in layers

The factory is one Claude Code plugin with layers that do different jobs. People decide. The qa-story session (headless: the driver, written in code) moves one ticket through ten stages and runs each of the twelve roles in its own session. Scripts and hooks check every transition, run the commands, keep the facts and build the projections. A fresh conductor is called only when the factory has no move of its own. A role never decides whether it may proceed, and a person is never asked what code can compute.

Layer 1 — people

Gate A and gate B are the planned decisions. The factory puts a question to a person at seven kinds of boundary: the review of the story's sources (s1_review), a gap in those sources (s1_gap), gate A, gate B, a halt (a concrete blocker with what is needed and from whom), the one closing question of the retro, and a question the conductor raises. Every one is a closed question: each answer is followed by what it costs the factory, what it costs you and what it does to the story. A text that breaks the rules of references/human-questions.md is still shown, and recorded as the factory's own defect.

Both gates share one protocol: one message, paths first, approval asked as its own item, the words recorded whole, and every block a person's word has already passed repeated back to them. The factory waits with no deadline. Delivery has three outcomes: ready to ship, ready once you finish the checks left with you, and not ready to ship as is. The outcomes and what each opens are on the overview page.

The S1 source review

qa-scout returns a table of the sources it found, with the factory's decision on each; you agree, name a ticket to flip, or say what to do about a source that is unavailable, and your words go to the scout. Every kind of gap after that is one closed question: not needed, skip all like these (it covers every later gap of that kind), a route, or the content pasted in. What the factory's own probe established it cannot reach (no route, no tool, too large) is a gap line in the report, never a question, and gate A says so with the door to try again.

A person's word passes any block

A check stops a silent pass, never an informed one. Your word passes any block, and the pass is recorded with your words, the block it passed and who said it. A pass over a block the factory owns is also filed as a finding about the factory. A ban is never a dead end: it names its door, and your word is one.

You write words; you read no script. A person's words travel in a file and are kept whole. The factory or the conductor puts them under state/words/ in the story's folder and hands the file to the command that records them (--words-file), so a multi-line answer is never cut. The conductor records only what you named, every item and nothing you did not say. Audit A24 exists so that no refusal on the dispatch path ships without a registered way for a person's recorded word to pass it.

Layer 2 — the driver and the roles

Besides the interactive session, a story can run without a chat: the driver story_run.py runs it headless, writes each question to a file in the story's folder and waits for your answer. It is described in design/driver.md and has no orchestrator model: each role is its own headless session in the project root (claude -p --agent qa-factory:<role> on Claude); a refusal meets a table of known refusals first and then the conductor; a gate, a halt, the S1 source review and the retro question are written to state/driver-question.md and the driver exits with 3; your words come back with --answer <file>. The host is a flag, --host claude or --host codex: the loop, the files and the journals are the same, and only the command that starts a role differs. Two journals record the run: state/driver-runs.jsonl (every model call: role, session, turns, cost) and state/conductor.jsonl (every refusal and who handled it). Nothing decides in the driver: roles and scripts do. The interactive qa-story session has the same stages and gates; there the session itself dispatches the roles and asks you in the chat.

bash [--host claude|codex] [--env ] [--mr ] [--answer ]]]>

Every role's last word is a form, not a report: the driver checks the handover form the way the exit gate does, and asks the role once, by resuming it, when the form is missing. Roles are named by references/stages.json, the machine-readable authority that stage_guard.py, exit_gate.py and inject_context.py read.

Beside it sits controller.py, the lifecycle writer: prepare, initialize, dispatch, accept, checkpoint, rework, revise_plan, invalidate, gate-decision, stage-scope, release, recover and the read-only next. Every mutation carries an idempotency_key and an expected_revision. next returns exactly one action — wait_existing_job, resolve_return, resume_checkpoint, relaunch, await_gate, prepare_stage or complete. A dispatch nobody is doing is proved, not guessed: dispatch_liveness answers alive, abandoned, never_started, returned or unknown from proof, and nothing is closed under unknown.

The conductor

The conductor is a fresh model call, a claude -p with a new context, that the driver makes when the factory has no move of its own: a factory script refused something the driver's own table does not know, your words match none of the answers a question offered, or you answered and your words must be recorded. Every call starts from the refusal, the story's state and the files the refusal names; nothing carries over. It makes at most 20 turns in a call, and the driver calls it at most three times per boundary before asking you.

The conductor finds the way on: it offers every move that lets the work continue, with what each costs and what it does to the story, runs the one you choose with the factory's own commands, and records a finding. The conductor reworks nothing itself: a broken artifact goes back to the role that wrote it. Its answer is a closed set of options, each followed by three price lines (the factory, you, the story); the price words are data in references/conductor-actions.json. When its answer cannot be used, the driver shows the standing options retry, go on and stop.

Three more moves carry no word of yours: the words go to the role that asked, a fact of the story is set, and a source you left out before the run is read after all, on your own words only.

The conductor never records a gate decision, never edits application code, tests, cases or reports, and never writes to a tracker. It never opens or pushes a merge request unless the delivery prompt names it authorized by the gate B decision, and then the MR is a Draft. It never edits the plugin's files, never fabricates evidence (a missing fact is a question), never edits pipeline.json, controller.json, journal.jsonl or events.jsonl by hand, never runs the test suite or starts a role's session itself.

Layer 3 — code

Guards and hooks

Nothing here is a role's discretion; it fires on the matching tool call.

  • guard_pre_tool.py — one PreToolUse process for the app boundary (P-00 protected application paths), secret patterns, quiet runs, a read of a raw run log over 8 KB, and a read of any file over 30,000 bytes whole. All fail-closed: an unparsed event blocks the call.
  • stage_guard.py — seven rules over stages.json and pipeline.json: (1) order, (2) gate A recorded before any stage past S3, (3) the automation manifest free of unresolved[] entries without a closed-list reason before S6–S8 or a test-code commit, (4) delivery gated on the verdict word yes or after_checks, or a recorded deliver_with_risks, (5) a factory git commit names its own pathspec, (6) SendMessage reopens only a dispatch id this story recorded, (7) git stash push -u names its paths so the story's own untracked artifacts cannot go into the stash. A person's recorded word passes rules 1 and 2. The guard is fail-open outside a factory story: no pipeline.json means "this is not a factory story".
  • validate_artifacts.py — PostToolUse on every artifact Write/Edit: the artifact against its schema, and the regex list in references/detectors.json.
  • exit_gate.py — SubagentStop: a role cannot hand off while its required_outputs or its witness artifact are missing, and a stage's extra detectors are re-run on a clean handoff.

Event store and controller projections

state/events.jsonl is the append-only authority; state/controller.json and pipeline.json are projections of it, and nobody edits them by hand. --operation recover repairs a torn last line, rebuilds the projections and closes a settled dispatch through a journalled state_repair; corruption mid-journal blocks writes instead of guessing. The project's journal is qa-factory/records/journal.jsonl. It is the human-readable append-only record of delegated, accepted, rejected, gate, escalated, deliver_with_risks and external_write.

Runner, evidence, manifest and ledger

Every factory run goes through run_suite.py — never pytest directly, by the driver or by any role — with typed requests; reuse requires matching test, helper, dependency, source, runtime and environment fingerprints (execution_fingerprint.py), and stale or missing freshness is unverified, never green. The red probe runs in a disjoint copy of the tree prepared by red_sandbox.py. work/evidence/ holds raw logs, junit XML and screenshots, never retellings. work/automation-manifest.json is written through manifest_put.py or derived from the tests' own markers; s5_precheck.py is the automator's exit detector and its findings go back to the author before the critic sees the work.

ledger.py generates the checks ledger by code, never a role: every plan row and every test lands in exactly one state. What the person reads is traceability.py — one row per requirement × case × Then group, with the test, its level and a result from a closed human list (pass · bug · wait · blocked · not run · unreachable · deferred · not planned). A test gets pass only from a green result row of its own, recorded for the code that is on disk now: with no row it reads not run, and a green run of a test that was changed since is not a pass. A Then that waits behind another case's red check in the same test reads blocked, never a pass.check_delivery.py aggregates the checks into one verdict word of three: yes, after_checks or no. The verdict is decided from typed records, never from the text of a reason, and a kind the factory does not know counts as not ready. The records and their kinds are data in references/delivery-verdict-kinds.json. The factory's own bookkeeping is owned by factory, never by product.

external_writes.py is the only thing that writes to a tracker or a TMS: a role writes its local file plus a proposal, and this script executes only the exact authorized action, case descriptions after gate A, results, bugs, delivery and retirement after gate B.

Visibility and costs

cost_capture.py tails the host's transcripts and rewrites state/cost/<session>.json. A story has one price on every page, computed by one function (task_price.py) from two independent records; how it is computed, and how far to trust it, is on the dashboard page. factory_dashboard.py and metrics_projection.py are local, code-only projections over accepted events; rendering never invokes a model, a product test, a scheduler or an external connector, and missing evidence stays missing rather than becoming a successful observation.

Editing the installed cache is the one thing that looks like a fix and is not: the next install overwrites it, the repository never learns about it, and the factory then behaves differently on two machines. A project no longer carries its own copy of hooks, roles or skills at all — hooks are registered once, globally, by the plugin manifest — so the only durable place for a change is the source. When a marketplace source is a local directory, the install copies the directory as it lies on disk: .gitignore is a git rule, not a copy rule. Run python scripts/selftest.py --hygiene in the clone before installing, and python scripts/install_smoke.py from the installed copy afterwards.

An archive made by qa-collect says whether the installed package was modified, by comparing it with the fingerprint the release carries. The archive's manifest reads package_modified: yes only when a named file differs from the release file of the same version, no when the package equals it, and unknown whenever there is a doubt, for example when the installation has no release file. Editor and cache leftovers are listed apart and never count as a modification.

How a role gets its knowledge

A role is defined by its charter, agents/<role>.md: frontmatter (name equal to the file name, a description ending in "Dispatched by qa-story at S<n>.", tools, and disallowedTools containing both Agent and Task — roles do not spawn subagents) and six sections: Role, Inputs, Procedure, Outputs, Witness, Refusals. Audit A19 checks all of that, and that no role pins a model.

On top of the charter, inject_context.py delivers the rule layer at dispatch time. The map is references/inject-map.json:

  • Common to every role: references/tester-manifesto.md and references/common-rules.md.
  • Per role: its own slices plus the project files it needs. A plugin: path resolves against the installed plugin; a project: path resolves inside the project.

Slices. A reference stays one file per subject, so one fact has one owner (audit A21). What a role receives is a slice of it: the lead plus only the sections marked <!-- roles: qa-automator, qa-code-critic --> on the line directly above the heading. contract_slices.py --write renders references/slices/<role>/<subject>.md and the results are committed; --check keeps them honest (a stale slice is a red build), --report prints per-role injected bytes.

The caps. Audit A22 caps what one role is injected before its charter and its first tool call at 42,000 bytes. Audit A25 is the cap that binds: the whole weight one role is handed before its first tool call (charter, common injection, its slices and the brief) against one number, 58,000 bytes in the script today. Audit A9 is the other half — every file the map names must exist, and every qa-* role must be in the map, or the injection is silently dead.

The traceability chain

What the factory promises at gate B is one unbroken chain per requirement, and check_traceability.py walks it by code:

  1. Requirement — R-n in work/requirements-register.md, with its source and its observable-in-code column. Requirements come from every source: the description, comments, attachments, linked tickets, the diff and acceptance criteria where the ticket has them. The factory works by requirements, not by a checklist, so a ticket with none still has its requirements checked.
  2. Plan row — one or more rows of test-plan.md whose R column names it, each carrying risk, verification_depth, disposition and design_review_required.
  3. Case version — the case whose Requirements: R-1, R-2 and Plan row: TS-n lines name them, pinned by case_versions.py into state/story-case-links.json and state/case-versions/.
  4. Test instance — one test is <function>[<parameter>], for example test_per_partition[missing-assistant]. An instance names no case: the cases it proves are covers: [TC-003, TC-004] plus its pinned case-version links. The mapping is N:M — one case may take several instances, one consolidated instance may prove several cases — and each Then clause is bound to a test and an assertion line in then_bindings[].
  5. Attempt — an immutable run block, reduced by finalize_task.py into run-report.json.
  6. Review and proof — the code critic's findings file plus a red-probe receipt for every changed instance, except a red instance whose contradicts_requirement row explains it: that one closes its window with the row, and no probe is asked of it.
  7. Decision — a terminal outcome for each requirement: an executed automated test, an executed manual-agent case, a manual-human case with an evidence package, or an explicit pending_implementation / deferred_by_decision. There is no "probably covered".
  8. Delivery — the ledger row, the delivery verdict, and only then the MR, the bugs and the authorized external writes.

A requirement whose chain breaks anywhere is reported with the link that broke and becomes a "not verified" ledger row. The one way a broken chain becomes terminal is a person: a decision recorded with your words reads here as deferred_by_decision, with the gap still named beside your own words. Without that record, gate B recomputes a gap gate A already accepted and sends the whole pipeline round to ask the same question.

Code is never an oracle: an expected value comes from the requirements, and where they are silent the factory asks you, showing how it was before the change and how it is now. Until you answer, the expectation stays open and is reported as a gap, neither a green nor a red result.

The audit rules

python scripts/audit_factory.py is the executable half of calibration, and /qa-factory:qa-doctor runs it, except A26 and A27, which the package's own tests run. An empty result is printed explicitly ("no violations") — silence is indistinguishable from not running. The factory-owned checks (A1, A2, A9, A11, A12, A14, A19, A21, A22, A24, A25, A27) run against the plugin's own shipped files; the rest run against one project's state.

Design principles

PrincipleHow it shows up in the build
The factory calls the conductor for a problem, never to run a command.In any state it has no move for, the factory calls the conductor, which offers options with their price and consequences and runs the one you choose. It never hands you a command to run by hand and never passes silently. Audit A27 finds a state without a move outside the one function that answers it.
A check stops a silent pass, never an informed one.Every refusal on the dispatch path is registered in refusal_registry.py with the way a person's recorded word passes it and the test that proves it; audit A24 fails a refusal without one. A ban always has a door.
A person's word passes any block, and the session (headless: the driver) or the conductor records it.A role never records a person's word. What is recorded is your own words, only what you named, read back at the next gate; a pass over a block the factory owns is also filed as a finding.
The person is asked only what a person decides; otherwise they hear one sentence.An inconsistency in the factory's own state is repaired (--operation recover) and stated in one line with owner: factory, never handed over as something to fix. What a role said to another role, a guard's refusal that was worked through, a state repair — none of it is theirs to read.
A gate is the last stop before its next stage.gate_preflight.py rehearses the decision the controller will record before anyone is asked. A factory_fixable finding is repaired before the gate is shown; a finding that is the person's goes into the gate message. After the answer, nothing is asked or refused that could have been computed before it.
A stand is a fact with a fix, not a paragraph.A stand gap travels as a card with the exact fix by name (names only, never a value) and four answers, written <case id>: change, myself, run or accept.
The factory never hands a person its own defect.A defect of the factory is stated plainly with owner: factory and recorded as a finding, not turned into a task for the person who hit it. A fail-closed refusal that fires twice on different calls stops the story with a diagnosis the factory owns.
Three delivery outcomes, not two.The verdict is yes, after_checks or no. The report's “Left with you” names every item left to a person, how to check it, and the regression scope the plan recommends; a missing item blocks the report, not the verdict.
Code is never an oracle.A test whose expectation was taken from the implementation can only pass. The expected value comes from the requirements; where they are silent the factory asks you, showing how it was before the change and how it is now.
The factory works by requirements.Acceptance criteria are one source of requirements, beside the description, comments, attachments, linked tickets and the diff; a ticket may have none.
The factory's roles run on Sonnet or Terra, never on Haiku.A fix that holds only on a stronger model is not a fix.
Everything in the repository is English.Every file, message, question and dispatch is English whatever the ticket's or the conversation's language; scripts/selftest.py runs an English-only package check before a change lands.

What the factory never does

  • Never deploys anything. You say where an environment is reachable; the factory never brings one up.
  • Never writes to a tracker or a TMS directly. A role writes a local file plus a proposal; external_writes.py executes only the exact authorized action after the matching gate.
  • Never launches product-test commands outside the authorized runner. Automated execution goes through typed run_suite.py requests; manual captures use validated immutable blocks.
  • Never runs regression. It is recommended to the person, with reason, priority, prerequisites and commands — and the person runs it on their own stand.
  • Never modifies application code. It files bugs; guard_pre_tool.py blocks the write. The one exception is a stand-gap card answered change: the factory makes the change that card shows, on your word.
  • Never edits its own plugin. A defect of the factory is a finding about the factory, not a plugin edit made in a story.
