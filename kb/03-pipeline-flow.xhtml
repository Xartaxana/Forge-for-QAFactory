QA Factory — Pipeline flow and decision points

Where a story can go differently, stage by stage: who decides, what the factory can do there, and what you can answer. Every chapter opens with a fork schema. State as of 26.10.2026.

How to read this page

This page describes QA Factory version 0.10.2. You run a story by typing /qa-factory:qa-story with a ticket; the session takes it through S0–S9, dispatching the roles, and asks you in the chat only what a person decides. The examples on this page come from the reference run of TP1-13797 on 13.10.2026.

The examples on this page come from a run without a chat; in an interactive session the same stages and gates reach you in the chat. A run without a chat is described on the page QA Factory — Architecture.

DeciderWho or what
codethe factory's scripts
rolea role, in its own session
conductorcalled only when the factory has no move of its own; it offers the moves
youthe person who knows the product and the story

A closed question says what the factory decided, why it cannot derive the answer, and asks one question with a closed set of answers. Every question the factory asks you is a closed question: each answer is followed by what it costs the factory, what it costs you, and what it does to the story. The words of those price lines are fixed.

LineThe fixed words
the factory:nothing runs · a record only · a factory command runs · one more working session of a role · stages run again
you:nothing more · this answer only · work of yours outside the factory · checks left with you
the story:delivery waits · unchanged · not known

When the factory has no move of its own, its conductor offers you every move that lets the work continue, each with its price; in an interactive session the session itself plays the conductor. Your word passes any block, and the pass is recorded with your words. A pass over a factory-owned block is also filed as a finding. A ban is never a dead end: it names its door, and your word is one.

At any stage

|a refusal the factory knows| Fix[Repaired by code] Step -->|a refusal with no known move| Cond{Conductor} Ask{Your answer} -->|one of the offered words| Move[The factory runs that move] Ask -->|other words, in a run without a chat: the driver| Cond Cond -->|a move it may take itself| Step Cond -->|options with prices| Ask Step -->|a role needs a decision| Halt{your words / stop} Halt -->|your words| Step]]>

What happens here. A refusal the factory knows is repaired by code; one it has no move for goes to the conductor. The conductor offers every move whose precondition holds, with prices, runs the one you choose with the factory's own commands, and records a finding. It reworks nothing itself.

What the factory can do.

  • When the factory has no move of its own, its conductor offers you every move that lets the work continue, each with its price; in an interactive session the session itself plays the conductor.
  • A problem the factory cannot repair itself reaches you as a question with options and their prices.
  • The conductor never edits application code, tests, cases or reports, and never writes to a tracker. It opens a merge request only when gate B authorizes it, and then as a Draft.
  • You answer in the chat, in your own words; the factory keeps your words whole in a file.
  • If the next stage will not fit the session, the factory asks you for /compact or a fresh /qa-factory:qa-story with the same ticket, and nothing you already answered is asked again.
  • A text that breaks the question rules is still shown, and recorded as the factory's defect.
  • Three moves carry no token: resume_with_words, story_fact and revoke_exclusion.

S0 — Start and preflight

|more than one of mr, branch, commit given| Flags{mr / branch / commit / stop} Flags -->|one is kept| List Start -->|one place or none| List[List of sources not to read] List -->|read before any fetch| Ticket[Ticket read by API, or your file or paste] Ticket -->|the read fails| ReadQ{not needed / retry / paste / stop} ReadQ -->|retry or paste| Ticket Ticket -->|criteria section found, nothing read from it| CritQ{paste / go on / stop} Ticket -->|no criteria section| Warn[Warning line, shown at gate A] Warn -->|goes on| Stand CritQ -->|go on| Stand Ticket -->|read| Stand{Stand settled?} Stand -->|cannot be settled| StandQ{a stand name / go on / stop} Stand -->|selected, or not answering: checked later| Ready[Readiness check] StandQ -->|a name or go on| Ready Ready -->|something required missing| ReadyQ{retry / go on / stop} ReadyQ -->|go on| S1([S1]) Ready -->|ready| S1]]>

What happens here. S0 has no role: code reads the ticket, settles the stand and checks that the project is ready. The ticket is read by API, or from your file or paste; no model reads it, and an LLM agent is not a data source. The outputs are pipeline.json and readiness.json. QA Factory v0 covers functional testing only.

What the factory can do.

  • It reads the list of sources not to read before any fetch, with its own questions.
  • With more than one of merge request, branch and commit given, it asks which one to keep.
  • A ticket with no criteria section does not stop the story; the requirements are read from the description. The warning line shown at gate A names the headings looked for and says the requirements are read from the description, comments, attachments and linked tickets.
  • A criteria section from which nothing was read does stop the story: the factory's reader failed, not your ticket.
  • A stand that does not answer does not stop S0: the stand is checked before anything runs on it.
  • Every S0 question also takes stop as a whole answer.

S1 — Context and grounding

|sources decided| Review{agree / flip / unavailable} Review -->|flip or unavailable: your words| Scout Review -->|agree| Read[Kept sources read in full] Read -->|unreachable, the factory's own probe says why| Gap[Gap in the report, no question] Read -->|unreachable for another reason| GapQ{not needed / skip all like these / a route / the content} GapQ -->|not needed or skip all like these| Read GapQ -->|a route| Scout GapQ -->|the content in a fence| Read Scout -->|no implementation found| NoDiff{change / lives / ungrounded / stop} NoDiff -->|change, lives or ungrounded| Scout Read -->|bundle valid| S2([S2])]]>

What happens here. The scout (qa-scout) writes the context manifest, the context bundle and the implementation diff. The scout shows you the sources it found and what it decided about each, and asks you to agree with the table. Kept sources are read in full. On the reference run there was one source review, three gap questions and one conductor question.

What the factory can do.

  • A source the factory established it cannot reach is a gap in the report, not a question. Gate A shows it under “Not reachable by the factory — recorded without asking you”, with the door to try again.
  • Any other gap is one question that lists only the points still asked; a long list is cut and the whole list goes to the details file.
  • One answer about a kind of gap covers every later source of that kind in the story.
  • A source you left out before the run is read only on your own words.
  • When no implementation is found, you choose: give the change, name where it already lives, go on with the requirements only, or stop.

S2 — Understanding and size

|requirements tester| Reg[Register, readiness, understanding] Reg -->|a decision only you can make| Halt{your words / stop} Halt -->|your words| Reg Reg -->|too many open questions| Many{answers / go on / stop} Many -->|answers| Reg Many -->|go on: questions stay open| S3([S3]) Reg -->|ready| S3]]>

What happens here. The requirements tester writes the register of requirements from every source, not only from acceptance criteria. Acceptance criteria are one source among others, and a task may have none. The outputs are the register, its readiness record and understanding.md; the size is computed by sizing.py. On the reference run S2 stopped with four questions.

What the factory can do.

  • Where the requirements are silent, the question shows you how it was before the change and how it is now.
  • An expectation nobody confirmed stays open and is reported as a gap, neither green nor red.
  • When too many questions are open, the stage stops with all of them in one question: answer them, go on with them open, or stop. One open question that blocks stops the stage at its handoff whatever the number of open questions; your answers are recorded by code at once and laid over the plan.
  • A question you want to keep open until the report is answered with the line Q1: until the report (the question's own id); it is not asked again and the plan keeps its unanswered branch.
  • A role that cannot go on without a decision halts and asks for your own words.

S3 — Plan

|test strategist| Plan[test-plan.md and work/test-plan.json] Plan -->|the plan check refuses| Plan Plan -->|too many open questions| Many{answers / go on / stop} Many -->|answers or go on| Plan Plan -->|valid| S4([S4])]]>

What happens here. The test strategist writes one test plan for the story: test-plan.md for you and work/test-plan.json for code. It works from the requirements register, the context bundle and the project strategy. On the reference run the plan stage asked no question.

When the story has an implementation diff, S3 runs in two windows. In the first the test strategist writes no plan: it fills work/grounding.yaml over the grounding table and the blast radius that code builds (grounding_table.py, blast_radius.py); code checks that table for completeness (every changed hunk is labelled) at the end of the first window; in the second window it writes the plan. Off the diff, S3 is one window. Still planned: a check that the plan answers every risk and area of the table, and the lines that show you the refactoring share and the requirement coverage before the first test; until they ship, nothing is asked between the two windows.

What the factory can do.

  • A plan check (planning_contract.py) runs again on the role's output; a refused artifact goes back to its role.
  • The stop for too many open questions applies here too, and so does the stop for one open question that blocks. Your answers are recorded by code at once and laid over the plan; a question you keep open with Q1: until the report is not asked again.

S5 — Automation

|stand not confirmed| StandQ{retry / a stand name / go on} StandQ -->|retry or a name| GA StandQ -->|go on: tests written, none run on a stand| Win GA -->|stand confirmed| Win[Next window: automator writes and runs its tests] Win -->|precheck refuses| Win Win -->|code critic| Crit{Critic} Crit -->|rejected, limit not spent| Win Crit -->|rejected, limit spent| Lim{round / debt / fixed / stop} Lim -->|round| Win Lim -->|fixed: you changed the tests| Crit Lim -->|debt| Debt[Window closed as debt, Review gaps in the report] Crit -->|approved| Acc[Window accepted] Crit -->|approved, a red test that its manifest row says the requirement contradicts: no probe, no word of yours| Acc Acc -->|more windows| Win Acc -->|last window| S6([S6]) Debt -->|next window or S6| S6]]>

What happens here. Gate A comes before S5. The automator writes the tests window by window, and the code critic reviews each window. The outputs are the automation manifest and the evidence; the detector is s5_precheck.py.

What the factory can do.

  • If no stand is confirmed for the story, the factory asks you before anything runs on a stand. One word covers S5 and S6.
  • When a repair limit is spent, the factory shows you what is still unresolved and waits for your decision.
  • In a run without a chat, a spent window limit is put to you as priced answers: one more round, debt, fixed or stop; a round your word buys is exactly one. When nobody can repair the rejection, the answers depend on the cause: review, debt or stop; fixed, debt or stop; debt or stop.
  • A window closed as debt is listed in the report under Review gaps with what the critic asked. The debt is kept in work/review-debts.json.
  • A red test that the requirement contradicts closes its window with its own manifest row, with no word of yours: when the contradicts_requirement row names the failed instance (the case, the instance or partition, the Then) and the critic approved it, code records the gap contradicts_requirement:<instance>, no probe is asked of the red test, and S7 triages the red. A green test with such a row still owes its probe, and a red test that no row explains is told the one road that closes it: send the row through manifest_put.py.
  • One test that covers several cases may stop at one case's red assert. The Thens behind that assert are recorded as blocked_by_check and read “not proven”, never a pass, in the ledger and in the report; a case whose own run failed reads as failed with its own verdict.
  • On the reference run the conductor offered restart, go on and stop; code executed the restart.

What happens here. The failure analyst gives every failure one verdict from a closed list: APP_BUG, TEST_BUG, TEST_BUG:collection, ENV_ISSUE, FLAKY, APP_CHANGED or CASE_DEFECT. It writes work/triage.json and the bug drafts. With nothing to triage, code closes S7 itself.

What the factory can do.

  • A TEST_BUG, a TEST_BUG:collection or a FLAKY failure is repaired; APP_BUG, ENV_ISSUE, APP_CHANGED and CASE_DEFECT are not.
  • An APP_BUG gives a draft; no bug file is created at this stage.
  • When a repair limit is spent, the factory shows you what is still unresolved and waits for your decision. In a run without a chat that is a priced question: one more round, debt or stop, and there is no fixed here. A test left in debt is listed in the report under “Runs left failing”.
  • The factory does not pay for a third triage: if a failure still has no verdict, it asks you.

What happens here. Delivery has three outcomes: ready to ship, ready once you finish the checks left with you, and not ready to ship as is. The verdict is decided from typed records, never from the text of a reason, and a kind the factory does not know counts as not ready.

VerdictThe report says
yesVerdict: ready to ship.
after_checksVerdict: ready once you finish the checks left with you.
noVerdict: not ready to ship as is.

What the factory can do.

  • Ready once you finish the checks left with you means the factory found no bug and no gap, but part of the testing is left with you.
  • It covers a handed test, a manual-human case, an accepted stand gap, a deferral you worded, a test the code critic could not verify, an unconfirmed expectation, a Then that waits behind another case's red check in the same test and a test that was changed after its run. A failure outranks a hand-off.
  • “Left with you” names each item with how to check it, and the regression scope.
  • A merge request is only ever opened as a draft, and only when the gate B decision authorizes it. The guard opens on yes and after_checks; the conductor never writes to a tracker.
  • In the reference run the gate B options named this outcome, but the recorded verdict was not ready to ship as is, because one requirement was not proven. The option line read “ready after your checks”; the report said delivery_ready: no.

S9 — Closure and retro

|retro mode full: the default| Q{Closing question} Q -->|your answer| Retro[Retro analyst writes retro.md] In -->|numbers or skip: only by your words| Short[Numbers only, or no retro] Retro -->|done| End([Story closed]) Short -->|done| End]]>

What happens here. The retro analyst writes the retro from the story's journal and cost records. The output is retro.md. S9 has one closing question for you. On the reference run the retro question was answered and the story closed.

What the factory can do.

  • The retro is full unless you ask for numbers only or skip by name. Silence or anything else stays full.
